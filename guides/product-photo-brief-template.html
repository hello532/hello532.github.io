Product photo brief template

Updated 4 September 2026 · reading time about 6 minutes

Retouching jobs go wrong in one of two ways: the brief did not say which image is the main listing image, or it did not say what must not change. Both are fixable in ten lines of text before you order.

Before you write the brief

  • Shoot on the real product. Phone shots are fine. Even good retouchers cannot invent a surface they have never seen, and a composite that flatters a product you do not actually ship converts once and then gets returned.
  • Decide the main image. It is the one doing the work in ads, search results, and listings. Name it in the brief and let the rest be secondary.
  • Know your platform's requirements. Aspect ratio, minimum pixel dimensions, and background rules differ by sales channel. Put the exact numbers in the brief instead of "high resolution".

The "must not change" block is the one that saves money. Retouchers optimise for a clean, attractive image. Left unconstrained, that means smoothing away the exact grain, weave, or brush finish a buyer is paying for — and a listing image that oversells texture is a returns problem, not a design problem.

Check on delivery

Acceptance checks to run on delivered images, and how to run each one
CheckHow
Colour is trueOpen the delivered file next to the physical product under daylight, not next to your original shot.
Edges are cleanZoom to 200% on the cutout edge. Look for halos, chewed corners, and lost fine detail such as hair, fabric fuzz, or thin handles.
Transparency actually worksDrop the PNG onto a dark background. Grey fringing shows up instantly.
Dimensions match the specCheck pixel dimensions and aspect ratio against the platform requirement you wrote down, not against "looks fine".
Source file includedConfirm you received the layered original if you asked for it. Without it, the next small change is a new order.
Mobile reality checkView the main image at listing-thumbnail size on a phone. If the key feature disappears, the crop is wrong regardless of quality.

Red flags while choosing a seller

  • A portfolio of only glossy electronics when your product is textile, food, or handmade. Ask for samples in your material class.
  • No before-and-after pairs. Finished images alone say nothing about what they started with.
  • Vague delivery list. "All files" is not a deliverable; enumerate formats and sizes.
  • Unwillingness to state whether the layered source file is included.
  • A promised turnaround that is dramatically shorter than everyone else's for the same scope.

What pushes the price up

Image count, composite complexity, whether a layered source file ships, rush delivery, and the number of output crops. Ask for a quote against the finished brief above rather than a rate per image — the brief is what determines the real scope.
