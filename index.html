What to outsource when you launch a store — and how to brief it

Most first-time store owners lose weeks doing work they were never going to be good at, then buy the wrong version of it from a freelancer because the brief was vague. These guides fix the second half: what to hand off, what to send with it, and what to check before you release payment.

The 7-task launch outsourcing checklist The only tasks worth paying for before your first sale, each with a decision rule for doing it yourself instead. Vetting a video editor for paid ads How to tell an ad editor from a wedding editor, what a first ad cut should contain, and the revision terms to agree up front.

How these guides are written

  • No price tables. Freelance rates move constantly and vary by seller, scope, and turnaround. Instead of stale numbers, each guide explains what pushes a quote up so you can read a live listing yourself.
  • No seller endorsements. Nobody here has bought from every seller in a category, so the guides teach vetting criteria rather than naming names.
  • Briefs you can paste. The most expensive part of outsourcing is rework caused by a thin brief. Every guide ends with something copyable.
