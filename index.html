What to outsource when you launch a store — and how to brief it

A first-time store owner loses weeks doing work they were never going to be good at, then buys the wrong version of it from a freelancer because the brief was vague. These guides fix the second half: what to hand off, what to send with it, and what to check before you release payment.

How these guides are written

  • No price tables. Freelance rates move constantly and vary by seller, scope, and turnaround. Instead of stale numbers, each guide explains what pushes a quote up so you can read a live listing yourself.
  • No seller endorsements. Nobody here has bought from every seller in a category, so the guides teach vetting criteria rather than naming names.
  • No invented statistics. If a claim would need data behind it, it is either sourced or not made.
  • Briefs you can paste. Rework caused by a thin brief is what makes outsourcing expensive. Every guide ends with something copyable.
