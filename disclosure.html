How this site is funded

Two ways, and they are different in kind, so they are stated separately.

One: paid work, done by me. The services page sells four engineering jobs — Shopify store data automation, a custom MCP server, price and stock monitoring, and a named-defect page-speed fix — at fixed prices I am named and held to. Payment is normally made directly by bank transfer. The first package may instead be booked through Contra, which handles that platform transaction under the terms shown at checkout. This is currently the only revenue path that has ever been open on this site.

Two: affiliate commission, not yet earning. The guides link to freelance marketplace categories where launch work can be bought, and the intention is to earn commission on qualifying purchases through those links. As of today no link on this site carries any tracking, so this path has earned nothing; the current state of both programs is set out below.

The conflict this creates, stated before you read any guide. These guides tell you how to judge a freelancer and what to demand on delivery, and I sell four of the jobs they discuss. That is a conflict of interest and no wording removes it, so here is what is actually done about it:

No guide recommends me. The offer lives on its own page, reachable from the navigation and from one card on the homepage. No guide's body text routes you to it, and none of them was written or rewritten to make buying from me look like the answer. Three of the four jobs I sell — store data automation, MCP servers, monitoring — are not taught anywhere in the guides. The fourth overlaps and is named here rather than left for you to notice: the landing page brief tells you how to make page weight a scope item with a builder, and the launch checklist lists page speed as something to have checked — and I sell a page-speed fix. Neither guide was edited to route you to it, neither names me, and both still tell you to verify the numbers yourself before and after, with the same tool both times, which is the standard my own package is held to.

The vetting criteria apply to me too. Everything the guides tell you to demand of a stranger — an enumerated deliverable, a named turnaround, source files you own, written terms before payment — is what the services page commits to. If it fails its own tests, that is a defect you can point at, not a difference of opinion.

If a $20 app does the job, you will be told. A scope reply that says "do not hire me for this" is cheaper for both sides than a refund.
Current status, stated plainly. Two marketplaces, two different states, and no tracked link on the site yet.

Upwork. An affiliate account exists, created on 4 September 2026 through Impact, the platform Upwork runs its affiliate program on. No tracked Upwork link has been placed on this site and none will be until three questions are answered in writing by Upwork. First, their affiliate terms let them withhold payout for "any referral or traffic to Upwork Sites from search engines", and this site is read almost entirely through organic search. Second, the same terms require prior written permission before posting "other content concerning the Upwork Offerings", which on a literal reading covers describing Upwork factually at all. Third, they prohibit promotional methods defined in a Master Campaign Agreement that has not been supplied, so the restricted list cannot be read. Until those are answered, Upwork is mentioned here as plain text with untracked links only. Their terms also pay out only on a first purchase by someone located in the United States, so for most readers of this site an Upwork referral would earn nothing even if it were tracked.

Nothing on this page is written to imply a partnership that does not yet exist.

When affiliate links are live, the arrangement works like this: if you click through and make a qualifying purchase, this site may receive a commission from the marketplace. Your price does not change. Commission is paid out of the marketplace's revenue, not added to your order.

What this site will not do

  • No invented prices for other people's work. Freelance rates change constantly and vary by seller, scope, and turnaround, so the guides explain what moves a quote and link to live listings instead of printing a table that goes stale. My own prices are on the services page as fixed numbers, because those are the one set of rates I can actually be held to.
  • No fabricated capability claims. The evidence offered for my own work is a public repository anyone can run: shop-mcp, 189 self-test assertions, and 23 deliberately injected defects that the suite catches. Those numbers are reproducible on your machine in one command, which is the only reason they are quoted.
  • No seller endorsements. Nobody here has purchased from every seller in a category. The guides teach vetting criteria instead of naming specific sellers as guaranteed choices.
  • No fabricated results, screenshots, or testimonials. No income claims, no invented case studies, no "clients" that do not exist.
  • No earnings promises. Outsourcing well reduces rework. It does not guarantee that a store sells anything.
  • No pressure tactics. No countdown timers, fake scarcity, unauthorised discount claims, or coupon codes that were not officially issued.
  • Cold email, stated plainly rather than denied. I do send unsolicited first emails to stores, so the honest version is what bounds it. Addresses come from the store's own published contact page or from the advertiser's public listing — never bought, never harvested from a private source, and never a personal inbox where a business address is published. Each email names defects I measured on that store's own live page and prints the command to reproduce them, because an unverifiable cold email is worth nothing to either of us. One first email per site and at most two follow-ups, scheduled 4 and 11 days after it, and every one of the three carries a line telling you how to stop the rest. Reply STOP, or say no in any wording, and the lead is closed on the spot: both queued follow-ups are killed and nothing further is sent, which is a state in the database rather than a promise about my intentions. There are no purchased lists.
